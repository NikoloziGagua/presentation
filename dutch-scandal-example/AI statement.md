# AI Usage Statement

> **Sample for comparison only.** This shows the tone and structure of a short, honest statement from a student who used AI lightly. A real submission has to describe what you actually did, so edit it to match your own use before relying on it.

**Tool used:** an AI chat assistant (Claude), free version.

## How I used it

1. **Choosing an angle.** I asked it to summarise the five ethics topics in a few lines each and say what each one means for developers. I picked the Dutch childcare benefits scandal myself, because it's a government algorithm case and I thought most developers wouldn't know the nationality field was part of the problem.

2. **Finding leads.** I asked it which organisations had reported on the scandal. That gave me names to search for, such as Amnesty International and the Dutch data regulator. I then read those sources myself and took every number and date from them, not from the AI.

3. **Proofreading.** I pasted my finished video script in and asked it to flag grammar mistakes and estimate the speaking time. I accepted a handful of small wording fixes and rejected the ones that didn't sound like me.

## What I did not use it for

The poster design, the layout, the "one column became a verdict" idea, the three lessons for developers, the closing question and the narration are my own. I made the visuals myself and credited the icons.

## Reflection: how useful was it?

It was useful for the boring parts. Getting oriented on five unfamiliar topics took minutes instead of an evening, and the proofreading caught typos I'd stopped seeing.

It was much less useful for the part the brief actually asks for, which is revealing something developers don't already know. When I asked for the key lessons, I got generic advice such as "be transparent" and "consider ethics", which is exactly what the brief says to avoid. The specific angle, that a single feature can be the whole problem, had to come from reading the sources and thinking about it myself.

I also learned to be careful with facts. It gave confident, round numbers without sources, and when I asked for references some of the titles didn't match anything I could find. After that I treated everything it said as a lead to check, never as a citation.

Overall I'd use it again for orientation and proofreading, but not for content or ideas. My voice and my argument were better without it.

*(About 370 words.)*
