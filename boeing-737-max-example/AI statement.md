# AI Usage Statement

**Tool used:** Claude (an AI assistant by Anthropic).

## How I used it

1. **Poster layout and design.** I used AI to help build the poster itself. It set up the A1 page, the 2×2 panel layout, the colour scheme, the icons and the big-number strip under the title. It also drafted the short text inside each panel from the sources I was working with. I then checked every fact against those sources and changed what I disagreed with. Getting a poster to look good at A1 is a design skill I don't really have, so this is where AI saved me the most time.

2. **Ideas for graphs and photos.** I asked it what visuals would explain MCAS to developers. It suggested comparing the 0.6° limit the FAA reviewed with the 2.5° that actually flew, which became Figure 3, and showing MCAS as a few lines of simplified code, which became Figure 2. It also suggested the kinds of photos to look for: the grounded fleet, the angle-of-attack sensor and the victims' families.

3. **Fixing problems.** When I opened the first version, the header photo was stretched and the slide was the wrong orientation for A1. I asked AI to fix both.

## What I did not use it for

I wrote the video script and the narration myself, in my own words, and recorded the video myself. I chose the Boeing 737 MAX topic myself, along with the "bug or business decision?" question it's built around.

## Reflection: how useful was it?

It was very useful for the visual side. Laying out a poster with this many elements by hand in Google Slides would have taken me days, and the first versions I tried myself looked cluttered. AI helped me get something that matches what our lecturer asked for: more images, less text.

It was less reliable on detail. The first layout had text overflowing its boxes, the wrong page orientation and a stretched photo, so I had to review it carefully and ask for fixes. I also couldn't trust the facts blindly. I checked the numbers and dates against the House committee report, the Seattle Times and the AP timeline, and only cited those.

The biggest lesson was that AI is fast at making something *look* finished, but I still had to understand the topic well enough to know whether it was actually right. That's also the point of the poster: code (or a tool) doing exactly what it was told isn't the same as it being correct.
