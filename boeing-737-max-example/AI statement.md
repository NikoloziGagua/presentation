# AI Usage Statement

> **Sample for comparison only.** This shows the tone and structure of a short, honest statement from a student who used AI lightly. A real submission has to describe what you actually did, so edit it to match your own use before relying on it.

**Tool used:** an AI chat assistant (Claude), free version.

## How I used it

1. **Understanding the technical side.** I'm not an aviation person, so I asked the AI to explain angle of attack, stabiliser trim and what MCAS was meant to do, in plain language. That helped me follow the investigation reports, which assume a lot of background knowledge.

2. **Finding sources.** I asked which investigations and news outlets had covered the crashes. That pointed me to the US House committee report and the Seattle Times reporting. I read those myself, and every number, date and quote on the poster comes from them, not from the AI.

3. **Checking my code figure.** I wrote the simplified MCAS pseudo-code in Figure 2 myself, then asked the AI whether it misrepresented how the system behaved. It pointed out that MCAS used one sensor at a time, not always the left one, so I changed the wording.

4. **Proofreading.** I pasted my finished video script in to check grammar and estimate how long it would take to read. I kept a few small fixes and ignored the ones that didn't sound like me.

## What I did not use it for

The "bug or business decision?" angle, the poster layout, the four panels, the chart, the three questions for developers and the narration are my own. The photos are ones I collected, and the icons are credited.

## Reflection: how useful was it?

It was most useful as a patient tutor. Getting the aviation basics explained quickly meant I could spend my time on the part that matters for developers: the single sensor, the changed requirement and the hidden feature.

It was much less useful for the argument itself. When I asked what the lessons were, it gave me generic points such as "test thoroughly" and "communicate clearly". The idea that MCAS wasn't really a bug, and that the code did exactly what it was designed to do, came from reading the House report.

I also had to watch it closely on facts. It sometimes mixed up dates and numbers between the two crashes, so I checked everything against the sources and only cited those.

I'd use it again as a tutor and proofreader, but not to write content. The poster is stronger because the argument is mine.
